# Case reading

Extract from a judicial opinion what it actually decides, on what reasoning,
and within what limits — the close-reading method of professional case
analysis. Method distilled in part from "How to Read a Case," Anne Coughlin,
University of Virginia School of Law (2015), whose worked example, _People v.
Bowen_ (Mich. Ct. App. 1968), illustrates several steps below. Use
`jurisprudence` for reasoning discipline and `general` for research, citation
and verification methods; treat every proposition extracted here as still
requiring verification for currency and treatment.

## Purpose

State why this case is being read before reading it: the precise doctrinal
question it must answer for the task (e.g. "how does this jurisdiction define
the actus reus of attempt"). A case that does not speak to that question is
set aside, not force-fit. In a synthesis or brief, record what each retained
case is a vehicle for; in teaching materials, infer the purpose from the
surrounding unit before reading.

## Caption and posture

Read the caption as data, not decoration:

- **Parties.** Party names and order signal the nature of the action — a
  sovereign as plaintiff signals a criminal or enforcement matter.
- **Court.** Identify the court's place in its hierarchy before assigning
  precedential weight, and never from its style alone: the Michigan Court of
  Appeals is intermediate; the New York Court of Appeals is that state's
  highest court. An intermediate court's rule statement warrants more caution
  than the same statement from the court of last resort.
- **Date.** Age cuts both ways — a fresh on-point precedent is strongest, but
  a foundational older case may still control. Either way, date drives the
  currency check.
- **Cause of action and procedural posture.** Identify what was litigated and
  where in the litigation the decision sits — verdict and appeal, motion to
  dismiss, interlocutory review. Posture fixes the standard of review and
  therefore what the court's statements can mean: "the evidence was
  sufficient" after a conviction is a deferential holding, not a factual
  finding.

## Facts

The stated facts are a curated narrative: the court selected them, and which
ones matter cannot be known until the legal analysis is read. Read the facts
once closely, read the analysis, then return to the facts and mark which ones
carried weight. Keep the court's characterizations distinct from the
underlying events — rhetorical distance ("apparently engaged in
conversation") is itself information. Retain a concise neutral statement of
what happened alongside the court's version.

## Arguments

Courts answer questions brought to them; they do not volunteer observations.
Where the opinion never lists the appellant's claims, reconstruct them from
what the court rejects — a sentence like "there was sufficient evidence to
support the conviction" exists because a party argued the opposite. Count the
distinct claims and track each to its disposition; a reader drawn to the
interesting issue routinely misses that another claim was decided first
(_Bowen_ resolves a sufficiency claim against the defendants before reaching
the instruction error they win on).

Reconstruct losing arguments in their strongest non-frivolous form — appellate
opinions are not written over frivolous claims — and identify who advanced
each theory the court entertains or rejects, including positions introduced
only as "it has been suggested."

## Rule and application

- **Elements first.** Fix the elements of the claim or crime as the court
  states them; every later dispute attaches to a specific element.
- **Instructions and operative texts.** Where the decision turns on a jury
  instruction, contract clause, or statutory text, read that text as the
  crystallized law of the case — an erroneous instruction on an element is
  the error, and its exact wording (what it required, what it omitted, what
  it cut short) is the holding's subject matter.
- **Anchor precedent.** Identify the binding authority the court applies and
  state its test in general terms, together with any application heuristic
  the court supplies (_Bowen_: conduct must "speak for itself" —
  unequivocally refer to the crime when viewed as a silent film, without the
  intent evidence).
- **Test slippage.** Verify that the authorities the court marshals apply the
  same test it announces. Cited cases sounding in a different formulation
  (proximity language inside a res-ipsa discussion) are a finding about the
  holding's stability, as is any disclaimer like "no rule can be laid down
  applicable to all cases."
- **Value judgment.** Extract the court's stated normative ground for the
  rule where given (_Bowen_: leaving room for abandonment — "the devil may
  lose the contest albeit late in the hour") and note documented rival
  positions (the corroboration view of the overt act, adopted by the Model
  Penal Code school). The rationale is what future courts weigh when the
  facts differ.

## Holding and limits

State the holding precisely — the disposition on each claim, tied to the
element and test that produced it — and how multiple holdings interact
(_Bowen_: the evidence was sufficient, yet reversal followed because the
instruction let the jury convict on an artificially truncated slice of the
conduct). Stay within the four corners: questions the opinion raises but does
not answer are recorded as open questions, not resolved by inference.

From each case retain: the doctrinal rule; the fact patterns held to fall on
each side of the line, for comparison against new facts; the normative ground
and its rivals; and the court, date and posture that bound the holding's
weight. Test understanding by varying the frame — apply a rival test to the
same facts, or read the facts as a different consumer of the rule (trial
judge, prosecutor, defender, arresting officer) — and record where the
outcome would change.

## Discipline

Tie every extracted rule, quotation and fact to its citation, locator,
jurisdiction and access date, and verify currency and subsequent treatment
before relying on any holding. Keep outputs under the established
`legal-references/{ST}/` workspace paths and carry unresolved checks into any
brief. This is research method for educational and personal-research use, not
legal advice, and no analysis here authorizes filing or external contact.
