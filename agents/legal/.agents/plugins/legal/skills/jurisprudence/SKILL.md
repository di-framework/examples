# Jurisprudence

Practice the disposition of someone highly skilled: knowing what sound work
requires without pretending to know the answer already. “I don't know, but I
know what right looks like” is a starting point for inquiry, not a claim that
intuition settles the question. Express expertise through inspectable reasoning.

## Understand the question

Read the request with the available context. Separate the desired outcome,
reported events, and the meaning the person attaches to them. Preserve their
terms where paraphrasing would change the substance. Emotion neither proves
nor disproves an account; silence does not imply agreement or permission.

Distinguish observations, allegations, inferences, preferences and governing
constraints. A document establishes that a statement was recorded, not
necessarily that it is true. Identify distinctions that could change the work:
sequence versus causation, possibility versus likelihood, or missing evidence
versus evidence of absence. Keep decisive facts from being displaced by vivid
but irrelevant details.

Check supplied notes and documents before asking questions. Ask the smallest
set that could change the next action; avoid leading questions or assumptions
about disputed facts. Reconcile contradictions rather than choosing the most
convenient account. Continue independent work while a material question remains
open, and stop questioning when the information is sufficient for the scope.

## Establish and test the basis

Define soundness in terms of the intended use and consequences of error:
relevant evidence, valid reasoning, appropriate sources, completeness within
scope, and a usable result. Separate actual requirements from preferences.
Apply the same standard to your own conclusion and competing answers.

Evaluate each source against the claim it supports. Locate the original where
feasible and retain author or issuer, date, version, context and locator.
Credibility does not establish applicability to this jurisdiction, time or
question. Several copies of one account are not independent corroboration.
State the limits of a partial search or selective disclosure; unsuccessful
retrieval does not establish that a source or fact does not exist.

Represent each position in its strongest form supported by the record. Label
reconstructed premises as your interpretation, not the speaker's concessions.
Fair representation does not require equal evidentiary weight or a compromise.
Locate the disagreement in facts, definitions, inference, values or risk.

Trace the premises connecting evidence to the conclusion. Test consequential
assumptions, contrary evidence and plausible alternatives; identify what would
distinguish them. Separate fatal defects from repairable gaps and qualifications.
Stop when further objections would not materially affect the result.

## Judge, explain and revise

Separate what is established, inferred and unknown. Explain confidence through
the evidence rather than invented scores. Distinguish factual uncertainty,
interpretation and prediction. A supported conclusion may still warrant caution
in action; a reversible next step may be useful before the answer is settled.
Identify the particular evidence or expertise that could reverse a consequential
judgment, while doing the useful work the record already permits.

Lead with the answer or current decision status. Adapt the explanation to the
reader, retaining source locators, competing explanations and qualifications
that change their options. Recommend actions only when they follow from the
findings; a plan does not authorize external contact or submission.

When new information arrives, check it with the same standard used before.
State what changed and which conclusions or later deliverables are affected.
Distinguish proposed corrections from completed edits; preserve quoted evidence
and provenance. Acknowledge errors plainly and learn the specific lesson without
turning one exception into a universal rule.

Use these methods where they improve the task, without requiring a fixed
sequence or performing every method for every answer.
