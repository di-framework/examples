# Civil procedure

Analyze where a civil dispute can be heard, what stage it is in, which
procedural rules govern the next step, and what each party must do or risks
losing. Procedure is analyzed against the actual court, posture and dates in
the record; a rule that is right for the wrong forum or the wrong stage is
wrong. Frame conclusions in IRAC/CREAC form and use `jurisprudence` for
reasoning discipline, `general` for research and citation methods, and
`constitutional` when a procedural question becomes a due process or
federalism question.

## Locate the governing rules

Identify the forum before citing any rule. Federal courts apply the Federal
Rules of Civil Procedure, the district's local rules, and the judge's
standing orders; state courts apply that state's rules, which may differ
from the federal pattern in numbering and substance. Do not transpose FRCP
doctrine into a state forum, or a general rule over a controlling local rule
or scheduling order, without confirming the state or court actually follows
it. In federal diversity cases, separate procedural rules from substantive
state law under the _Erie_ doctrine and mark close classifications as
contested rather than assuming an answer.

## Analytical sequence

Apply the steps the question requires, in order, and record which were
reached:

1. **Posture.** Establish the current stage from filed documents and docket
   entries: pre-suit, pleadings, discovery, dispositive motions, trial,
   post-judgment or appeal. The available motions, standards of decision and
   deadlines all depend on it.
2. **Forum power.** Test subject-matter jurisdiction (federal question,
   diversity with amount in controversy, supplemental jurisdiction),
   personal jurisdiction over each defendant (consent, presence, long-arm
   reach within due process limits), venue, and service of process.
   Subject-matter jurisdiction cannot be waived and can be raised at any
   time; distinguish it from waivable defenses and note removal and remand
   where both state and federal forums are possible.
3. **Pleadings.** Evaluate claims and defenses against the governing
   pleading standard — plausibility under _Twombly_/_Iqbal_ in federal
   court, the forum's own standard elsewhere — plus heightened requirements
   for fraud-type claims. Track amendment rights and limits, compulsory
   versus permissive counterclaims, and joinder of claims and parties.
4. **Motions.** Match each motion to its stage and standard: dismissal for
   the defenses the rules enumerate, judgment on the pleadings, summary
   judgment on the absence of genuine disputes of material fact, and trial
   and post-trial motions. State who bears the burden, what record the court
   may consider, and what an adverse ruling forfeits or preserves.
5. **Discovery.** Analyze scope through relevance and proportionality,
   privilege and work-product protection, the duty to preserve once
   litigation is reasonably anticipated, and the tools available —
   disclosures, interrogatories, document requests, depositions,
   examinations, admissions. Tie disputes to the meet-and-confer,
   protective-order and sanctions framework the forum actually imposes.
6. **Judgment and appeal.** Identify what makes a decision final and
   appealable, interlocutory exceptions, the preservation steps an appeal
   requires, and the standard of review each ruling will receive. Analyze
   claim and issue preclusion from prior judgments before proposing any new
   filing.

## Deadlines

Treat every deadline as a claim requiring its trigger, its counting rule and
its source. Record the asserted basis — rule, statute, court order — and
compute under the forum's counting method, including weekend, holiday and
service-method adjustments, only when the trigger date and rule text have
been inspected. Distinguish jurisdictional time limits from extendable ones.
Where a trigger, applicable rule or tolling fact is unverified, report the
deadline as unverified with what is missing; a wrongly computed limitations
or appeal deadline is the kind of error that cannot be repaired later.

## Discipline

Tie every procedural proposition to the inspected rule text, order or
authority with citation, locator, jurisdiction and access date; rules and
local rules are amended on regular cycles, so confirm the version in force
for the relevant period. Distinguish what the docket shows filed from what a
party reports, and what a rule permits from what a scheduling order has
already narrowed. Keep outputs under the established `legal-references/{ST}/`
workspace paths and carry unresolved checks into any brief. This is research
method for educational and personal-research use, not legal advice; it does
not calculate a controlling deadline for reliance, authorize a filing, or
substitute for local counsel.
